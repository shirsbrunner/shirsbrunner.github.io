## Let me ramble like the old guy I am

When I was younger, I listened to the radio every day. Radio was a constant companion with music, quizzes, tales and news. Growing up I switched away from the parents channel listening to one intended for a younger generation. 

But eventually, a time came, when I mainly listened to records, huge collections of mp3s. We were sharing discs and collections among friends. Even on the road, where radio was bettern than the boring tapes, a disc man, or an Ipod was catering to the taste better than whatever was on air. Most probably it was also availability. Noone was using a small portable radio...

Scrobbeling stuff to last.fm was insightful for comparing taste in music and to analyize my own behaviour. Listening to the current favourite song 20 times in a row? Indeed.

## But listening to canned music on demand also has its drawbacks

When listening to music, I prefer LP's and MP3's.  I still use CD's, albeit rarely. Those are from that phase, when CDs went and MP3 grew. Music that I never ripped and then forgot in the box – I don't really have an optical drive handy.

Luckily, in Switzerland, public radio allows you to download a lot of podcasts of stories and tales to listen in your own time. 

> Sidenote: I will never understand people who want to defund public television and radio. Why would you want to listen to often not so stellar content riddled with adds the whole time? 

At work, outside of client ecosystems, I used to listen to music on youtube, where I have a lot of playlists. The time spent at clients offices was brief enough for the limited MP3-world.

After it being a constant companion, nowadays I only listen to the radio, when I don't wake up before the alarm. And even then, it's probably on for around 5 seconds a day before I turn it off to get up.

## The appeal of curated music experiences

Why stop listening to music on demand? Apparently... after a certain age, people don't really change taste in music anymore. I guess it happens, because of canned music, music that you own. We stop exploring, people don't share mixtapes anymore, we are not exposed to a diverse taste anymore. Well, maybe it's just me or my generation.

Eventually, listening to the same thing over and over gets boring.

I never got the appeal of spotify or similar services. It's like radio, but missing the diversity of content, and not paying artists well enough. 

How to embrace the exploring again and why would you change your habits? Suddendly, you find yourself with a kid that brings home new music and wants to explore a developing taste, while you get sidecar experience of learning, what's cool nowadays. 

In my case, it's also switching jobs into a more restricted ecosystem. Working as an inhouse designer has its advantages and its drawbacks. Not working on my own machine I'm hesitant on just using youtube, and I have to have the phone active, else the stream stops (because google want's to sell an upgrade).

## The appeal of radio
I don't know about you, and maybe I'm a romantic, but radio is ticking all boxes for me.
